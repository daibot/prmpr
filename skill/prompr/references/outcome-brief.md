# Outcome brief

The brief is the contract. Everything later gets checked against it. Keep it to one screen.

## Fields

| Field | What good looks like | Weak version to push back on |
|---|---|---|
| Brand / product | Exact product, offer, and what is new | "The brand" |
| Outcome | One behaviour or belief change: "book a test drive", "see us as the safe choice for first-time parents" | "Awareness", "engagement" |
| Audience | Who, plus one truth about them that the work can use | Demographics only |
| Insight | A tension the audience feels that the brand can resolve | A fact about the product |
| Single-minded proposition (SMP) | One sentence, one benefit | A list of features |
| Reasons to believe | Proof points that are real and approved | Invented stats |
| Tone | 3 adjectives plus one "not": "warm, wry, confident, not jokey" | "Fun" |
| Channel and formats | Where it runs and the sizes or lengths | "Everywhere" |
| Mandatories | Logo, CTA, legal, URL, required copy, do-nots | Left blank |
| Success | How the team will judge it, ideally a metric | "Client likes it" |

## Turning vague into usable

If the outcome is vague ("awareness"), offer a sharper version and ask them to confirm:
"Awareness of what, among whom? Would 'make lapsed users remember we now deliver in 20 minutes' be right?"

If there is no insight, propose two candidate insights based on the audience and category and let them pick.

## Assumptions

When you assume something, list it under **Assumptions** at the top of the deliverable so the team can catch it fast.
